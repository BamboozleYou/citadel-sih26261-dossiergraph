# Strong engineering, crowded field, unproven edge

Our SIH26261 idea earns an **overall 6.5/10**. I estimate its chance of reaching the Grand Finale at **about 5–10%**. That is roughly three to five times the national base rate of about 2%, but it is still far more likely to be screened out than shortlisted. The idea fits the problem statement well and is technically more specific than most submissions. It loses most of its marks on novelty, because the public SIH26261 field has already converged on almost the same feature set. **Nirikshan**, one public repo, already implements APH rules with clause citations, four-level document authentication (metadata, noise forensics, pyHanko signatures/QR, a mock issuer registry), salted-hash ghost-faculty checks, pHash photo-reuse detection, an institution self-check and fully on-prem LLM use ([Nirikshan](https://github.com/amanray10/Nirikshan-SIH26261)). Most of our "stand-out" list is therefore table stakes. Three things remain clearly differentiating: the synthetic forgery benchmark with measured precision and recall, the no-code rule builder with dry-run impact preview and two-person approval, and the depth of the security engineering. Of the three, only the benchmark maps onto a criterion judges are known to score. The plan also has a presentability problem. It contains about 40 named features and a 25-item tech stack, and the official template allows only 6 slides including the title, reviewed without the team present ([SIH template copy](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1UAcsy0OUn86ZzrcGbXTdcyoc6uqV4Jr2/htmlpresent)). Three uncertainties apply throughout. sih.gov.in returned HTTP 403, so official per-PS submission counts, the official rubric and the 2026 dates could not be verified. Third-party sources put the idea-submission deadline at **30 September 2026** ([FirstVidya](https://firstvidya.com/sih-2026-guide/)), so the PPT has probably been submitted already, and the improvements below would then mainly help if we are shortlisted. Finally, I have assumed the submitted deck reflects the feature plan.

## Roughly 1 in 50 ideas reaches the finale, and SIH26261 is crowded

The selection round is a numbers game before it is a quality game. SIH 2025 drew **72,165 idea submissions for 271 PSs and sent 1,360 teams to the finale**, which is about 1.9% overall and about 5 finalists per PS ([newsonair](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/8th-edition-of-smart-india-hackathon-2025-begins-mic)). Third-party guides say each PS locks at 500 submissions and that about 4–5 teams per PS reach the finale ([FirstVidya](https://firstvidya.com/sih-2026-guide/)). SIH26261 looks to be one of the busier PSs. A GitHub search on 26 Oct 2026 found **23 repos matching "SIH26261" and 77 matching "26261"**, against about 5 each for the sibling AICTE PSs SIH26262 and SIH26265 ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=SIH26261&type=repositories)). Public repos are a small fraction of all entrants, so the realistic picture is several hundred ideas competing for about five slots.

Screening is done on the PDF alone. A 2025 winner described the national stage as partly luck because evaluators review the deck without the team present ([Kartikay Singh](https://medium.com/@kartikaysingh4329/how-i-won-indias-biggest-hackathon-sih-2025-69dafd1d792e)). An unsourced blog claims evaluators spend 2–3 minutes per deck and suggests weights of innovation 25%, problem understanding 20%, feasibility 20%, impact 20% and presentation 15% ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/sih-2026-ppt-template-exact-format-slides-evaluators-score/)). No official national rubric exists publicly. However, every college rubric and the template's own slide headings converge on the same axes: novelty, PS fit, technical approach and feasibility, impact, and clarity. The template itself instructs that the "idea must be unique and novel" ([SIH template copy](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1UAcsy0OUn86ZzrcGbXTdcyoc6uqV4Jr2/htmlpresent)). **No rubric found lists security or privacy as a criterion** ([MCKVIE 2023](https://www.mckvie.edu.in/pdf/SMART%20INDIA%20HACKATHON%20Report%202023.pdf)). This matters directly, because our plan names security as its primary differentiator.

## What the plan gets right that rivals may not

It is worth noting where the idea earns its 8.5 on fit, because those strengths are real. The plan correctly targets the actual gap. AICTE approval is already fully online through the AICTE portal and NSWS. It rests on self-disclosure, with deficiencies listed in EoA letters and fixed within six months *after* approval ([AICTE Public Notice](https://aicte.gov.in/sites/default/files/Public%20Notice%202025-26.pdf); [VESIT EoA 2026-27](https://vesit.ves.ac.in/storage/aicte/1775193851EOA%20Report%202026-27%20(2).PDF)). A pre-scrutiny triage tool fits that gap better than "digitising approvals." The realism section avoids claiming "detects forgery," avoids blockchain, and labels mocks honestly. That is exactly right, because DigiLocker requester access is gated by NeGD approval, needs India-hosted servers and has no sandbox ([DigiLocker onboarding FAQ](https://www.digilocker.gov.in/assets/FAQ%20DL%20EL_onboarding.pdf)), and judges have tired of blockchain pitches ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/hackathon-ideas-that-won-2026-judges-actually-pick-examples/)). Human-in-the-loop with four-eyes approval matches the "People First", "Accountability" and "Understandable by Design" sutras of MeitY's AI Governance Guidelines ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/e-governance/digital-india/india-ai-governance-guidelines?lgn=en)). The DPDP transparency feature is timely, since the main fiduciary obligations take effect around **13 May 2027** ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). These strengths help with feasibility and risk (slide 4) and with trust (slide 5). They do not create novelty.

There is a steelman for the security-first strategy. Security-centric solutions have won SIH before, for Defence and MEA PS owners ([Amrita Cyber Sena](https://www.amrita.edu/news/cyber-sena-of-amrita-wins-top-award-at-smart-india-hackathon-2018); [YourStory 2019](https://yourstory.com/2019/07/data-security-system-smart-india-hackathon-student)). An AICTE evaluator who handles faculty PAN and Aadhaar data might appreciate it. But those wins came on PSs where security *was* the problem. Here the PS owner asked for validation and authentication, and a competitor's deck will promise "on-prem, hashed IDs, audit trail" in one bullet. Our ten-row table looks deeper but scores about the same.

## Highest-leverage changes, in priority order

If the deck has not yet been locked, or for the finale if we are shortlisted, these changes move the score most per hour of effort.

First, **make the synthetic forgery benchmark the headline**. Build it now. Generate a labelled set of synthetic dossiers with altered dates, swapped seals, reused photos and incrementally saved PDFs. Report per-indicator precision and recall, plus the reviewer false-positive burden (flags per genuine dossier). Put the number on slide 2. It is the one thing no public rival shows, it answers the PS's evaluation section, and it defuses the "ELA is noisy" objection. This alone would plausibly lift novelty from 4.5 to about 6.

Second, **replace every placeholder with real APH 2024–27 clauses**. Encode 15–20 real rules and cite the official handbook ([APH PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s35938b4d054136e5d59ada6ec9c295d7a/uploads/2025/03/2025031399.pdf)). Nirikshan already does this, so a `Clause x.y` placeholder is an immediate credibility loss against it.

Third, **add the signature feature the field lacks: a dossier consistency graph**. Link the land-deed survey number to the building plan, the plan to the fire NOC address and occupancy, and the NOC to the declared built-up area and intake. The research ranks this as the most defensible remaining gap, and no KYC vendor does it ([market notes; IndiaAI on IDfy](https://indiaai.gov.in/article/idfy-deploys-ai-to-detect-fraud-and-clear-the-fog-of-deceptiveness)). It replaces generic name/address fuzzy matching with something only an AICTE-specific tool would do.

Fourth, **compress security into one "trust layer" box** on the architecture slide. Map it to the MeitY sutras and DPDP, and keep three demoable items: the tamper-evident log, HMAC matching and four-eyes. Remove Kubernetes, Prometheus, Bhashini, LayoutLM and the DevSecOps list from the slides. Present a clearly scoped 36-hour MVP (OCR, rules, the consistency graph, PDF forensics, pyHanko, the benchmark, the evidence viewer) with everything else on a roadmap line. This raises feasibility and presentability together.

Fifth, **add issuer-first trust tiers and build the DigiLocker mock to the real spec**. Auto-trust documents that are DigiLocker-issued or DSC/QR-signed, and run forensics only on uploaded scans. Model the mock on the real OAuth 2.0 PKCE requester flow ([Requester API spec](https://entity.digilocker.gov.in/assets/img/Requester%20-%20Entity%20Locker%20API%20Specification_28_11_24.pdf)). AICTE has mandated NAD/DigiLocker uploads since 2021 ([AICTE-NAD circular](https://cdn.digilocker.gov.in/nad/assets/circulars/4%20Circular_AICTE_NAD_DigiLocker_06_2021.pdf)), so this is a credible integration story.

Sixth, **reframe the rule builder dry-run around regulatory change**. Pitch it as "survives APH addenda and the VBSA transition," citing the pending Bill ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/education/news/shiksha-adhishthan-bill-single-higher-education-regulator-ugc-aicte-125121500767_1.html)). That turns a nice feature into a risk-mitigation argument on slide 4.

Seventh, **consider remote EVC tooling** if capacity allows. The APH lets the Expert Visit Committee verify "offline or online" ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/163708613/)). A geotagged, timestamped capture flow matched against declared infrastructure was rarely seen in rival repos. Avoid "Pramaan"-style names, since at least three teams already use one.

## Conclusion

Our idea is not weak. It is well-engineered and positioned around the wrong differentiator. The field has made forensics, APH rules and human-in-the-loop design the price of entry, so the remaining marks go to teams that can *prove* their system works and that model AICTE's dossiers better than anyone else. Our plan already contains the seed of the proof, the benchmark, but buries it beneath security features the rubric does not reward. Applying the first four changes would plausibly move the overall score from 6.5 to about 7.5 and the shortlisting odds toward the low teens. Even then, the base rate means no version of this deck makes shortlisting likely.

If the September deadline has passed and the PPT is fixed, the outcome now depends on how the deck was compressed. The useful work is to build the benchmark, the real-clause rules and the consistency graph anyway. They are what would separate us in the finale's 36-hour evaluation rounds, where judges reward an integrated, working prototype and how teams respond to their feedback ([Vansh Rathi](https://medium.com/@vkrvanshrathi/sih23-the-road-to-the-grand-finale-of-smart-india-hackathon-25869c133734)).
